Cryptic Pocket Benchmark

TDA-EGNN compared with the other cryptic binding-site predictors I could find, grouped by how fairly each comparison can be made.

Bottom line

  • Strictly cryptic proteins (the one comparison run under a single protocol): per-protein AUC is 0.627, level with P2Rank (0.608) and with the benchmark authors’ own model (0.643), and above the ESM-based and cavity-feature models. The clear gap is at the top of the list: 0.209 precision in the top 20 residues against P2Rank’s 0.084.
  • Whole CryptoBench test set: AUC 0.911–0.918 is the highest I found published, MCC 0.466–0.470 is level with Seq2Pocket (0.460–0.473), and AUPRC 0.431–0.448 is below Seq2Pocket’s 0.475, which was measured on a set with a higher share of pocket residues.
  • Averaged per protein instead of pooled: AUC 0.863 against 0.816 (UniPocket) and 0.799 (ACF).
  • Not claimed: that this is the best method on cryptic sites. There are only 16 matched proteins, the AUC intervals span about 0.3, and differences among the top three are within noise. Every number outside the first section comes from another paper’s own setup.

2. CryptoBench test split, pooled over residues

The standard way CryptoBench results are reported. Each paper filters the test set a little differently, so differences of a few hundredths are not decisive.

AUPRC rises with the share of positive residues, so rows with a higher positive rate are favoured on that column. F1 is omitted: the CryptoBench papers use a class-weighted F1 that is not comparable to a plain positive-class F1.

Different averaging

3. CryptoBench test split, averaged per protein

These papers average a metric over proteins instead of pooling residues, which rewards small proteins as much as large ones and gives lower AUCs. Your model was rescored this way for the first row.

4. PocketMiner’s test set

How to read these numbers

AUC is the chance that a real pocket residue is ranked above a non-pocket one (0.5 is guessing). AUPRC is stricter on rare positives. MCC scores the yes/no call (0 is guessing, 1 is perfect). Precision@20 is the share of the model’s 20 highest-ranked residues that are real pocket residues, the list a chemist would actually inspect.

Limits of this benchmark. P2Rank’s training data cannot be audited, so overlap with these proteins cannot be ruled out. Seq2Pocket, ACF and the cryptic-site benchmark are preprints, not peer reviewed. Older methods such as CryptoSite use different datasets and were not rescored. AE-PocketMiner reports beating PocketMiner but its numbers could not be read from the preprint.
